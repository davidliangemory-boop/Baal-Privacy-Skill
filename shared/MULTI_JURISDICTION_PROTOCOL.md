# Multi-Jurisdiction Synthesis Protocol — v0.4

Use this protocol when the business question spans two or more jurisdictions.

## 1. Normalise the Business Question, Not the Law
Start from common factual questions, for example:
- What data is collected?
- From whom?
- For what purpose?
- On what legal basis?
- Is sensitive data involved?
- Who receives it?
- Does it cross borders?
- What notice/consent/filing/localisation is required?

Then apply each jurisdiction's own doctrinal structure.

## 2. Preserve Local Legal Tests
Do not translate every local concept into GDPR Article 6/9 vocabulary. Record:
- local statutory term;
- local legal test;
- local regulator/court interpretation;
- nearest functional analogue only if useful.

## 3. Comparison Output
For each jurisdiction distinguish:
- `COMMON_BASELINE`
- `LOCAL_OVERLAY`
- `STRICTER_REQUIREMENT`
- `UNIQUE_REQUIREMENT`
- `UNCERTAIN_POINT`

## 4. Global Control Rule
A global control may be recommended when it is operationally efficient, but clearly separate:
- legally mandatory minimum in each jurisdiction;
- chosen global standard;
- business policy exceeding local law.

## 5. Conflict and Localisation
Where local law conflicts with a global policy, local legal requirements prevail for the affected population/system unless counsel decides otherwise. Flag legal incompatibility rather than averaging the regimes.
